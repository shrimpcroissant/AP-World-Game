01Short answers

When I click End turn, what happens?
The enemy refills its units, draws, and places cards one at a time. Then the battle runs: one whole side acts, then the other side acts. That is the whole round. Then it is your turn again. Details
In round 1, do all my figures act before any enemy figure?
Yes. Every one of your figures acts, then every enemy figure. Next round the enemy side goes first. It alternates every End turn.
Does movement all happen before attacks?
No. There are no separate move and attack phases. Each figure takes one action when its turn comes, either a move or an attack, and it resolves right away before the next figure acts.
Does a figure always attack if it can, and otherwise move?
Each figure looks at exactly one tile: straight ahead (diagonal for a Jumper). Enemy there: it attacks. Empty: it moves in. Your own figure there: it waits. You never steer figures; this is automatic.
If my front figure is blocking one behind it, does the front one move first?
Yes. Figures act front-most first, so the front one moves and the one behind steps into the tile it just left, in the same phase. Example 1
If I go first and step next to an enemy, did I just walk into a hit?
Yes. Moving uses your action, so the enemy side then hits you. Next round the enemy goes first, so it hits you again before you swing. Who hits first
Do moves happen at once? Who wins a tile two figures want?
Nothing is simultaneous and nothing is random. The first figure to act takes the tile; the later one finds it full and attacks (if it's an enemy) or waits (if it's a friend). Example 4
Do attacks happen at once?
One at a time, in the same front-most-first order. Damage lands instantly, and a figure that dies is removed before the next figure acts, so it never gets its own action that round. Defenders never hit back out of turn.

02The board

Four lanes (A to D) and five rows. You place in row 1 and your figures walk up. The enemy places in row 5 and its figures walk down. A lane is a column; figures never leave their lane except the Jumper.

Row bonuses are live

A figure gets +1 HP while standing in its own two home rows and +1 attack while standing in the other side's two home rows. The middle row gives nothing. The bonus is recalculated from where the figure stands right now, so it disappears the moment the figure steps out. A wounded figure that is only alive because of its home-row HP dies as soon as it walks into row 3 (Example 10).

03Your turn

  1. Units refill. Turn 1: 4. Turn 2: 5. Turn 3 onward: 6. Plus 1 for every lane you are winning, meaning you have strictly more figures in that lane (any row) than the enemy. Unused units do not carry over.
  2. Draw, if you want. Click the deck once per turn to draw 1 card plus 1 for every 4 units you have (4 to 7 units: 2 cards; 8 to 11: 3 cards). Drawing stops at 7 cards in hand. You can skip drawing and just play your hand.
  3. Place cards. Pick a card, then an empty tile in row 1. It costs the card's units. Place as many as your units and empty row 1 tiles allow.
  4. Use items or the Dagger at any point in your turn (see Items).
  5. End turn. Click the token or press Space. From here everything is automatic until your next turn.

Hover a figure on your turn to see a NEXT TURN −X or DEATH preview. The preview plays out the coming battle with the figures already on the board. It cannot know which cards the enemy is about to place, so it can be wrong when the enemy drops a new figure in front of you.

04What End turn sets off

The steps run in this order, each about 0.2 seconds apart (three times slower in the tutorial).

  1. Enemy units refill: its base (Easy 3, Normal 4, Hard 5, final fight at least 6) plus the same turn ramp you get (+1 on turn 2, +2 from turn 3) plus 1 per lane it is winning. If its deck is empty it skips steps 2 and 3.
  2. Enemy draws 1 card plus 1 for every 4 units, up to 7 in hand.
  3. Enemy places cards, one per step, into row 5 until it can't afford a card or row 5 is full. There is no separate enemy End turn; once it can't place, the battle starts. (How it chooses.)
  4. First side acts. Every figure on that side, one at a time, front-most first.
  5. Second side acts. Same rules. Lane lights are checked after each side finishes.
  6. Your turn starts. Who goes first flips for next time.

Back-to-back actions

Because the order flips, the side that acted last in one battle also acts first in the next. Read across two battles the sequence is You, Enemy | Enemy, You | You, Enemy. Your only chance to change anything between those two enemy actions is your own turn of placing cards and using items.

05How one figure acts

When its turn comes, a figure looks at exactly one target tile: the tile straight ahead, or for a Jumper the tile diagonally ahead. What is in that tile decides everything.

Target tile isEmptyMoves into it.
Target tile isAn enemyAttacks it, does not move.
Target tile isYour own figureWaits. Nothing happens.
Target tile isOff the boardNothing happens.
  • One action per figure per battle: a move or an attack, never both.
  • No counter-attacks. A figure that gets hit does not hit back until its own side's phase.
  • Extra steps (Runner, Arabian Horse, Cloth) only happen right after a successful move, only into empty tiles, and never attack.
  • Artillery is the exception: it never walks or attacks normally. See The five figures.

06Order inside a side

Within one side, figures act one at a time, front-most first: the figure nearest the opponent goes first. For you that means the highest row first. For the enemy it means the lowest row first. Lanes don't matter; only distance forward does.

Two figures in the same row: the code does not promise left-to-right or any fixed order between them not fixed. It only matters when two same-row figures want the same tile, which in practice means a Jumper.

Example 1A column advances together

Two of your Sailors stand in lane B, one behind the other, with nothing ahead.

If the rear Sailor acted first it would see a friend ahead and wait. Front-first ordering is what lets a column march as a block.

Example 2The front figure is fighting, so the one behind waits

Your front Sailor has an enemy directly ahead. Your second Sailor is behind it.

Example 4Two of your figures want the same tile

A Jumper in A2 is heading right, so its target is B3. A Runner in B1 wants to move 2 tiles: B2, then B3.

The Jumper is in row 2 and the Runner in row 1, so the Jumper acts first and takes B3. The Runner's extra step finds a friend there and stops. Enemy and player figures can never fight over an empty tile in the same moment either, because the two sides never act at the same time. Whoever's phase comes first takes the tile, and the other side then finds an enemy there and attacks it.

07Who hits first

This is the part that decides most fights. Moving uses up a figure's action, so the figure that steps into contact never gets to attack that same phase. The other figure is now standing right in front of it and hits first.

Example 3One empty tile between two Sailors

Your Sailor in B2, an enemy Sailor in B4, and B3 empty. Same setup, two different battles.

The rule this shows

With one empty tile between two figures, whichever side acts first walks into the other's attack. In the second case the enemy Sailor also stepped out of its home row and lost its +1 HP on the way in, which made it even easier to kill.

Why it can be two hits, not one

Take a tougher figure of yours that survives a hit, and say contact happens during a battle where you act first. The order of swings looks like this:

Battle 1 · youYou move into contact
Battle 1 · enemyEnemy hits you (hit 1)
Battle 2 · enemyEnemy hits you again (hit 2)
Battle 2 · youYou finally swing

Flip it around and the enemy pays the same price. If contact is made by the enemy stepping toward you, you get two swings before it gets one. So the strongest place for your figure is often two rows short of an enemy at the end of a battle, letting the enemy close the gap.

You can't tell a figure to hold still, but you control timing: when you place, which lane, and items like the Zebra and Stone Tablet that stop enemy placement.

Sailor

The plain rule from How one figure acts, nothing more. Examples 1 to 3 are all Sailors.

Jumper

  • Moves one row forward and one lane sideways. Every Jumper, yours or the enemy's, starts out heading toward lane D.
  • If the next lane over would be off the board, it turns around and heads the other way in that same action. It keeps the new heading afterwards, and it turns even if it ends up waiting or attacking.
  • It only ever looks at its diagonal tile. It attacks an enemy there, waits for a friend there, and completely ignores whatever is straight ahead. Jumpers slip past blockers and can hit figures other figures can't reach.

Example 5A Jumper bounces and attacks on the diagonal

Artillery

  • Has a counter that starts at 0 and goes up by 1 every battle it is on the board. The battle right after you place it counts as the first.
  • On the 5th battle it fires: 1 damage to every enemy figure in its lane, any row, with no row bonus. It never hurts your own figures.
  • After firing, the counter resets to 0 and it steps forward 1 row if that tile is empty. It never attacks.
  • The rest of the time it is a 5 HP wall: enemies ahead attack it, and your figures behind it wait.

Example 6Artillery fires down the lane

Runner

  • Takes its normal action. If that action was a move, it takes 1 extra step into an empty tile. The extra step never attacks.
  • Finishing a lane: if the Runner stands in the second-to-last row (row 4 for you) and the far row tile ahead is occupied by anything, friend or foe, it doesn't attack. It finishes the lane as if it had arrived: the lane lights up and gets wiped, Runner included. This can trigger on its normal action or its extra step. If that lane is already lit, nothing happens and it just stays blocked.

Example 7Runner steps, gets blocked, and finishes a lane

Swordsman

  • Moves like the plain rule. When an enemy is straight ahead, it hits that enemy for its full attack and hits the tile directly behind it (two ahead, same lane).
  • An enemy in that second tile takes full damage. One of your own figures there takes half, rounded down (attack 2 or 3 both do 1).
  • The second hit lands even if the first target died. If the tile ahead isn't an enemy, there is no second hit.

09Damage and death

  • Damage applies the instant an attack happens. A figure dies when its current HP, home-row bonus included, reaches 0 or less.
  • A dead figure is removed immediately. Its tile is free for the very next figure, and it does not get its own action later in that phase.
  • Extra damage beyond what kills a figure does nothing.
  • Myrrh (your item, one use): the first time any of your figures would die, it survives on 1 HP instead. That includes lane wipes and losing a home-row bonus.

Example 10Dying from a step forward

Your Sailor in B2 was hit earlier. Its base HP is 0, and it is only alive on the +1 HP of your home row.

10Reaching the end of a lane

  • Above the board are 4 lights for the enemy's end. Your figure arriving in row 5 lights that lane's top light. Below the board are 4 lights for your end, lit by an enemy reaching row 1.
  • Lights are checked after each side's phase, before the other side acts.
  • Lighting an unlit lane wipes it: every figure in that lane, on both sides, including the one that arrived, takes damage equal to its HP and dies (unless Myrrh saves yours).
  • Arriving in a lane that is already lit: the figure simply leaves the board. No wipe, and it doesn't count as lost.
  • You win with 3 of the 4 top lights. The enemy wins with all 4 bottom lights, except in the final fight on the coast of Iraq, where it only needs 3. Lighting two lanes at once is fine; going from 2 to 4 still wins.
  • If both sides would win in the same moment, whichever light the game checks first decides it, and nothing overwrites that result depends on check order.

11How the enemy plays

  • Choosing a card: always its most expensive card it can afford. On a tie, the first one in its hand.
  • Choosing a tile: a random open tile in row 5. On Hard and in the final fight, it instead picks the lane where your figures have advanced furthest, breaking ties at random. A lane blocked by your Zebra is never chosen.
  • Easy: each turn there is a 25% chance it stops after placing 1 card.
  • Stone Tablet: it places nothing on its next turn.
  • Out of cards: it skips its placing for 3 turns, then reshuffles. The battle still happens every turn.
  • Its deck: 12 Sailors, 5 Jumpers, 3 Artillery, starting with 4 cards (5 on Hard). Enemy cards never expire.
  • Final fight (coast of Iraq): 6 Sailors, 4 Jumpers, 2 Artillery, 4 Runners, 4 Swordsmen. It starts with 6 cards, gets at least 6 base units, always targets your furthest lane, and needs only 3 of your lights to win. A warning appears at the start.
